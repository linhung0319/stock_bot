## 1. How large asset managers structure top-down and bottom-up layers

### Takeaway
Documented practice splits into two models: a **published house view** produced by a central institute on a fixed calendar (BlackRock: twice-yearly forum, weekly commentary) and a **no-house-view exchange** where macro, sector and stock insights are pooled daily but each decision-maker stays independent (Wellington, Capital Group). In both, the shared layer is an *input that is broadcast once to everyone*, not something each stock analyst re-derives.

### Cited Findings

**Wellington (no house view, daily shared meeting)**
- Wellington has "more than 800" investment professionals (as of 30 June 2025); insights are shared daily in Morning Meetings "connecting ideas and people across asset classes, investment disciplines and geographies". — [Wellington, Inside our Morning Meetings (PDF)](https://www.wellington.com/content/dam/wellington/pdf/en/morning-meeting-v7.pdf)
- Two-stage daily relay: an "Early Morning Meeting" at 8:30 am London time (Asia + Europe, on global markets); "a video replay and notes from this meeting are available to our US-based investors at the start of their day"; a second meeting at 8:30 am Boston time links London and the US. Thursdays are a single all-region meeting at 7:30 am Boston. — [Wellington PDF](https://www.wellington.com/content/dam/wellington/pdf/en/morning-meeting-v7.pdf)
- Topic selection is a triage step: "Topics are selected from comment cards submitted by participants before the meeting about a subject on which they have differentiated insights or that requires action." A moderator rotates weekly. — [Wellington PDF](https://www.wellington.com/content/dam/wellington/pdf/en/morning-meeting-v7.pdf)
- "We do not have a house view on any investment topic and instead pursue a 'one-firm' approach to examining the markets." Content "can range from analyses of corporate earnings to macroeconomic and policy trends to sector- and industry-specific assessments." — [Wellington PDF](https://www.wellington.com/content/dam/wellington/pdf/en/morning-meeting-v7.pdf)
- The meeting's stated output is follow-up work, not decisions: investors "debate the most important issues in the market, driving follow-up work and conversations" (Kim Gailun, Head of Equity Boutiques). — [Wellington PDF](https://www.wellington.com/content/dam/wellington/pdf/en/morning-meeting-v7.pdf)
- The tradition dates to 1958; more than 13,000 meetings held. — [Wellington PDF](https://www.wellington.com/content/dam/wellington/pdf/en/morning-meeting-v7.pdf)
- A 1998 profile described research as anchored by a global industry group of 31 analysts who evaluate investments on an industry basis and make stock recommendations to a range of portfolio managers. Dated; current structure unverified. *(search summary only)* — [IR Impact, Profile: Wellington Management Company (1998)](https://www.ir-impact.com/1998/05/profile-wellington-management-company)

**Capital Group (multi-manager, shared insight, independent decisions)**
- The Capital System combines multiple portfolio managers within one portfolio; managers share insights but make their own investment decisions. *(search summary only)* — [Capital Group, The Capital System](https://www.capitalgroup.com/advisor/ca/en/about-us/capital-system.html)
- Analysts are not only recommenders: they typically can invest in their highest-conviction ideas, and most funds have research portfolios managed by analysts. *(search summary only)* — [Capital Group, The Capital System](https://www.capitalgroup.com/advisor/ca/en/about-us/capital-system.html)
- Collaboration is described as spanning portfolio managers, analysts, economists and quantitative research teams. *(search summary only)* — [Capital Group, The Capital System](https://www.capitalgroup.com/institutions/ca/en/about/capital-system.html)
- Scale of bottom-up input: 20,004 company meetings and 16,382 pieces of proprietary research in 2023 (firm's own figures). *(search summary only)* — [Capital Group, Fundamental research](https://www.capitalgroup.com/advisor/about-us/our-beliefs/fundamental-research.html)

**BlackRock Investment Institute (published house view on a calendar)**
- Over 100 BlackRock portfolio managers, strategists and executives gather twice a year (two-day Outlook Forum) to debate economy, markets and geopolitics; the Global Outlook is described as the culmination of that debate. *(search summary only)* — [BlackRock Investment Institute](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/insights/blackrock-investment-institute/insights); [Market Take podcast, "Consensus forms at Outlook Forum"](https://chartable.com/podcasts/market-take/episodes/171065334-consensus-forms-at-outlook-forum)
- The 2026 Midyear Outlook states it captures discussions at the June 2–3 Forum. *(search summary only)* — [BII Midyear Outlook 2026 (PDF)](https://www.blackrock.com/us/individual/literature/whitepaper/bii-midyear-outlook-2026.pdf)
- BII publishes a weekly commentary; the 11 Nov 2024 issue is titled "Zeroing in on secular forces, not cycles", i.e. the weekly layer is framed around slow structural themes rather than daily news. *(title seen in search results only)* — [BII weekly commentary 2024-11-11 (PDF)](https://blackrock.com/ca/investors/en/literature/market-commentary/weekly-investment-commentary-en-us-20241111-zeroing-in-on-secular-forces-not-cycles.pdf)

**Bridgewater (daily macro research note; AI triage in front of analysts)**
- Bridgewater describes the Daily Observations as a way of giving clients and policymakers "a real-time view into how we're processing the world". *(search summary only)* — [Bridgewater](https://bridgewater.com/)
- AIA Labs (Bridgewater's AI research and investment lab) targeted "the initial step of information triage: identifying what is relevant and interesting to read" before analysts spend time. Details in section 3. — [Bridgewater AIA Labs, Learning to Replicate Expert Judgment in Financial Tasks (30 Jun 2026)](https://www.bridgewater.com/aia-labs/learning-to-replicate-expert-judgment-in-financial-tasks)

### Inferences
- Three reusable patterns for the bot's "composite layer":
  1. **Produce once, broadcast to all** (Wellington's replay-and-notes relay): one market-level note per period that every per-stock review reads, instead of each review researching the market itself.
  2. **Shared layer informs, per-stock layer decides** (Wellington "no house view", Capital "share insights but make own decisions"): the composite output should be context attached to each stock review, not an instruction that overrides the stock thesis.
  3. **Admission test for the shared layer** (Wellington comment cards: "differentiated insight or requires action"): an item enters the shared note only if it changes something for at least one holding or needs an action; otherwise it is dropped.
- Layer cadence gets slower as scope gets wider at BlackRock (semi-annual outlook, weekly commentary). A personal bot can mirror that: slow scheduled environment note plus a small event-triggered addendum.
- A natural layer-to-output mapping for the bot (inference, assembled from the above): market environment note (what regime, what big events) -> sector/theme notes limited to themes that holdings are tagged with -> per-stock review that receives only the notes whose tags it matches.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved for **Fidelity, T. Rowe Price**, or the internal workflow of **Bridgewater's Daily Observations**; nothing on these should be treated as documented here.
- Could not confirm from BlackRock documents whether portfolio managers are free to deviate from the BII house view (commonly stated, but not verified in this session).
- **Sell-side morning meeting structure** (analyst calls -> sales/trading, strategist and economist slots) and **CFA Institute material on investment committees** were not retrieved; no sourced findings.
- Capital Group items come from search-result summaries of the firm's marketing pages, not from full-page reads; they describe intent, not audited practice.

## 2. Thematic / exposure mapping from a macro or theme event to holdings

### Takeaway
Vendors map events to holdings through three data layers: granular industry classification with revenue share per segment, geographic revenue exposure, and customer/supplier relationship graphs. All three are paid; the underlying raw material is company filings, so a hand-maintained tag table for 10–40 holdings is a realistic free substitute.

### Cited Findings
- FactSet Supply Chain Relationships: relationships are sourced from primary sources and "reverse-linked" to counterparties that did not disclose them; four categories (customer, supplier, partner, competitor) with 13 sub-types. A 2014 note cited over 150,000 relationships, reviewed annually from SEC filings, history to 2003. *(search summary only)* — [HBS Baker Library, FactSet Revere Supply Chain Relationships](https://www.library.hbs.edu/databases-cases-and-more/datasets/factset-revere-supply-chain-relationships)
- FactSet RBICS with Revenue standardises the revenue percentage of each reported business segment into the most granular RBICS sectors; about 45,000 companies. *(search summary only)* — [FactSet, RBICS DataFeed at a glance](https://insight.factset.com/resources/factset-revere-business-industry-classifications-datafeed)
- FactSet GeoRev gives a geographic revenue breakdown beyond what financial statements disclose. A FactSet tariff piece combines GeoRev + Supply Chain + RBICS to surface indirect exposure (e.g. non-US firms with heavy US revenue that depend on Chinese suppliers). *(search summary only)* — [FactSet Insight, Tariff uncertainty: hidden revenue and supply chain risks](https://insight.factset.com/tariff-uncertainty-unveiling-hidden-revenue-and-supply-chain-risks?hs_amp=true)
- Bloomberg SPLC shows, for a centre company, the percent of revenue, COGS, CAPEX, SG&A and R&D that Bloomberg has quantified across customers and suppliers. Coverage is acknowledged as partial: data is "typically incomplete" because there is little mandated supply-chain disclosure. Bloomberg's current page cites 500,000 unique relationships. *(search summary only)* — [Emory Libraries, Bloomberg SPLC guide (PDF)](https://prod.libraries.emory.edu/sites/default/files/migrated-documents/db-pages/bloomberg-splc.pdf); [Bloomberg Professional, Supply chain](https://professional.bloomberg.com/institutions/corporations/supply-chain/)

### Inferences
- Minimal version for ~10–40 holdings (inference; free data only): one row per holding with (a) 2–4 business-segment tags with rough revenue share from the annual report, (b) revenue by region from the annual report, (c) 3–5 named key customers/suppliers/competitors, (d) 3–6 macro sensitivity tags (rates, USD, oil, China demand, AI capex, tariffs, etc.) with sign. This is the same three-layer shape as RBICS + GeoRev + SPLC, filled by hand or by an LLM reading the filing once per year.
- Routing rule (inference): an event in the composite layer carries tags from the same vocabulary; a per-stock review receives only events whose tags intersect its row. Events with no intersecting holding are logged but never routed.
- Since even Bloomberg calls its graph incomplete, the hand table should record "unknown" rather than guess; an LLM-filled table needs the source sentence stored beside each tag.
- Refresh cadence for the table can follow the vendor norm found above (FactSet reviews relationships annually) plus a refresh when a holding reports a segment change or acquisition.

### Gaps
- No vendor methodology document was found that publishes the exact allocation formula for geographic revenue estimates.
- **Theme baskets** (sell-side or index-provider thematic baskets) and formal **scenario/sensitivity analysis** process documents were not retrieved; no sourced findings.
- The free-data basis (US GAAP segment and major-customer disclosure rules, e.g. the 10%-of-revenue customer disclosure) was searched but not returned; it is not verified here.
- Paid-data flag: FactSet (RBICS, GeoRev, Supply Chain) and Bloomberg SPLC all require institutional subscriptions.

## 3. Event triage by scope and materiality

### Takeaway
Documented triage systems score each item on **relevance to the entity**, **novelty (new vs repeat)**, and **event category**, and the best-documented human-expert practice (Bridgewater) splits "relevant" from "interesting" as separate judgments. The SEC 8-K item list is a free, ready-made company-level event taxonomy.

### Cited Findings
- RavenPack: a relevance score measures how important an article is to a given company; academic work finds highly relevant articles move returns while low-relevance ones barely do. *(search summary only)* — [WRDS, RavenPack documentation](https://wrds-www.wharton.upenn.edu/documents/1395/RavenPack.pdf)
- RavenPack novelty metrics indicate whether a detected event is new, a repeat, or a continuation of an existing event or theme; events sit in a hierarchical taxonomy (one source cites 5,000+ categories) detected with template programs. *(search summary only)* — [Celent, RavenPack overview](https://www.celent.com/solutions/676764665); [WRDS, RavenPack documentation](https://wrds-www.wharton.upenn.edu/documents/1395/RavenPack.pdf)
- Bridgewater AIA Labs three-label triage: **relevant and interesting / relevant but uninteresting / irrelevant**. Example given: a small IPO is financially relevant but lacks "broad significance"; an article mixing geopolitics and finance can look relevant to a naive model but not to an investor. — [Bridgewater AIA Labs (30 Jun 2026)](https://www.bridgewater.com/aia-labs/learning-to-replicate-expert-judgment-in-financial-tasks)
- Same source, measured difficulty: frontier models with a plain task prompt averaged about 50% accuracy across six triage tasks; expert-written instructions and reframing lifted this to the mid-70s; best frontier model 78.2%; Bridgewater's fine-tuned model 84.7%. Investors wanted 80% before trusting it in daily work. Splitting "relevant but uninteresting" from "relevant and interesting" improved results. — [Bridgewater AIA Labs](https://www.bridgewater.com/aia-labs/learning-to-replicate-expert-judgment-in-financial-tasks)
- Same source: experts found these judgments hard to articulate, which limits what a prompt can carry; noisy non-expert labels were the main cause of poor performance. — [Bridgewater AIA Labs](https://www.bridgewater.com/aia-labs/learning-to-replicate-expert-judgment-in-financial-tasks)
- SEC Form 8-K items (free company-level taxonomy): 1.01/1.02 material agreement entry/termination; 1.03 bankruptcy; 1.05 material cybersecurity incident; 2.01 acquisition/disposition completed; 2.02 results of operations; 2.03/2.04 new or accelerated financial obligations; 2.05 exit/disposal costs; 2.06 material impairments; 3.01 delisting notice; 3.02 unregistered equity sales; 3.03 changes to holder rights; 4.01 auditor change; 4.02 non-reliance on prior financial statements; 5.01 change in control; 5.02 director/officer departure or appointment; 5.03 bylaw/fiscal-year changes; 5.07 shareholder vote results; 7.01 Regulation FD disclosure; 8.01 other events; 9.01 exhibits. — [SEC, Exchange Act Form 8-K C&DIs](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/compliance-disclosure-interpretations/exchange-act-form-8-k)
- Baker, Bloom, Davis and Sammon code every market jump on three axes: proximate cause (17 categories, including "Unknown & No Explanation"), geographic source, and clarity. This is a documented scope-plus-confidence schema for market-level events. — [NBER WP 28687 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28687/w28687.pdf)

### Inferences
- A minimal triage record for the bot (inference): `scope` (market / sector-theme / company), `category` (8-K item for company events; a short macro list such as monetary policy, fiscal, macro data, geopolitics, sector regulation, unknown), `novelty` (new / continuation / repeat), `relevance` (which holdings' tags it hits), `interesting` (does it bear on any thesis item or break condition). Only items that are new AND relevant AND interesting consume heavy-AI time.
- Bridgewater's roughly 50% baseline is a direct warning: a bare "is this news material?" prompt is near coin-flip. The practical lesson for a solo bot is to write explicit criteria with worked examples from the owner's own judgments and to keep a small labelled set to check the triage prompt against.
- Scope decides the layer: market and sector items go to the composite layer once; company items (8-K categories) go straight to that stock's review. This is what removes the duplicated market research on a broad sell-off day.
- 8-K items give a free materiality prior: 4.02, 1.03, 2.06, 5.02 (CEO/CFO), 2.02 and 1.01 are thesis-relevant classes; 9.01, 5.03 and 5.07 are mostly routine (ranking is inference, not from the SEC).

### Gaps
- RavenPack's own taxonomy file and scoring formulas were not read directly; findings rely on WRDS and Celent descriptions seen through search summaries. RavenPack is paid.
- **Bloomberg event categories** and **SASB materiality map** were not retrieved; no sourced findings.
- No documented taxonomy found for non-US filings (e.g. Taiwan MOPS material-information categories) in this session.

## 4. The post-hoc narrative problem

### Takeaway
The academic record says a substantial share of large market moves have no convincing news cause, that explanation clarity varies and is itself informative, and that media tone partly reflects past returns and predicts reversal. The best-documented handling is to code **cause + confidence + clarity + "unknown" as a legitimate answer**, and to consider flow/positioning mechanisms that only become measurable weeks later.

### Cited Findings
- Cutler, Poterba and Summers examined the 50 largest one-day S&P 500 moves of 1946–1987; many could not be tied to convincing news about cash flows or discount rates, and many of the largest moves occurred on days with no major news. — [NBER WP 2538](https://nber.org/papers/w2538); summary in [CBS News, What moves stock prices?](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/what-moves-stock-prices) *(search summary only)*
- Fair (2002) matched newswire events to large 1–5 minute S&P 500 futures moves, 1982–1999: 69 identified events, 53 of them related to monetary policy; "many large stock price changes have no events associated with them". *(abstract via search summary)* — [Fair, Events That Shook the Market, Journal of Business 75(4)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jnlbus/v75y2002i4p713-732.html)
- Baker, Bloom, Davis and Sammon: 8,049 daily jumps in 19 national markets (plus 455 US bond-market jumps); US threshold 2.5% up or down gives 1,179 jumps 1900–2023, which are 3.5% of trading days but nearly 20% of total absolute daily variation and half of quadratic variation. — [NBER WP 28687 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28687/w28687.pdf)
- Method: trained human readers code next-day newspaper articles; they record the journalist's explanation, "the confidence with which the journalist advances an explanation" and the ease of coding; clarity combines journalist confidence, ease of coding, pairwise agreement across reads, and whether any explanation is advanced. — [NBER WP 28687 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28687/w28687.pdf)
- "Over the past 90 years, the share of jumps due to unknown forces fell from about 35 percent to 10 percent in both the United States and the United Kingdom." — [NBER WP 28687 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28687/w28687.pdf)
- "Market volatility tends to be greater for at least two weeks before and after low-clarity jumps (as compared to high-clarity jumps)"; greater clarity also foreshadows less dispersion in firm-level returns. Jumps triggered by monetary policy foreshadow much lower volatility than other jumps. — [NBER WP 28687 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28687/w28687.pdf)
- Policy news (monetary, government spending) accounts for a highly disproportionate share of upward jumps, more so after a crash; newspapers attribute 38% of jumps in their own national markets to US developments. — [NBER WP 28687](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28687)
- Important limit: the paper's stated aim is to "accurately characterize and code the journalist's explanation and interpretation" — it measures what journalists said, not verified causation. — [NBER WP 28687 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28687/w28687.pdf)
- Tetlock (2007): high media pessimism predicts downward pressure on prices followed by reversion to fundamentals; tests reject the hypothesis that the media content contains new information about fundamental values; low market returns lead to high media pessimism. *(search summary only)* — [Tetlock, Giving Content to Investor Sentiment (PDF)](https://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock_Media_Sentiment_JF.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=685145)
- Flow/positioning explanation, worked example: BIS traced the early-August 2024 turbulence to a volatility spike and yen appreciation forcing leveraged carry positions to unwind, with "deleveraging pressures amid thin markets" as amplifiers. That bulletin is dated 27 Aug 2024, about three weeks after the 5 Aug move. *(search summary only)* — [BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.htm); [BIS Quarterly Review Sept 2024, Carry off, carry on](https://bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2409a.htm)
- Contemporaneous estimates of that unwind disagreed within days: a JPMorgan strategist put it at 50–60% complete on 6 Aug, and JPMorgan strategists said three-quarters was removed by 8 Aug. *(search summary only)* — [Fortune, 8 Aug 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/08/08/75-carry-trade-blamed-recent-stock-meltdown-unwound-jpmorgan/)

### Inferences
- The Baker et al. coding sheet is directly portable as the composite layer's output format for a sell-off day: `what happened` (index and sector moves, breadth — facts), then `candidate explanations` each with category, source, and stated confidence, plus an explicit `unknown / no clear cause` option and a clarity grade. With roughly 10% of modern jumps still coded unknown, and those codes being journalists' accounts rather than verified causes, "unknown" must be an allowed output and the model should not be forced to name a cause.
- Clarity is actionable on its own: low clarity goes with elevated volatility for at least two weeks either side. A reasonable bot rule (inference) is: low-clarity market drop -> expect more noise, do not re-underwrite individual theses on that day; re-check after a waiting window.
- Tetlock's result implies same-day pessimistic coverage is partly an echo of the price fall. So media tone should not be passed to per-stock reviews as evidence about fundamentals; pass facts (what was announced, numbers) and label interpretation separately.
- Flow explanations (deleveraging, carry unwind, crowding) are usually confirmable only from data published later (the BIS analysis arrived weeks after). So the same-day note should carry them as hypotheses with a "revisit on" date, and the stored record should be updated when better evidence arrives — a two-pass design: same-day provisional note, later settled note.
- The key per-stock question after a broad drop is therefore not "why did the market fall" but "did any thesis item or break condition for this holding change" — a question the market-level narrative mostly cannot answer. This is the reason to do market research once and keep it out of each stock's reasoning except as context.

### Gaps
- No documented professional-desk procedure was found for recording competing explanations with confidence or for mandatory waiting periods; the handling rules above are inference from the academic coding method, not observed desk practice.
- **Shiller's Narrative Economics** and **Tetlock (2011) "All the News That's Fit to Reprint"** (stale-news reaction) were not retrieved in readable form; no sourced findings.
- CTA / volatility-control / systematic selling mechanics were not covered by the BIS results returned; positioning data sources usable for free (e.g. CFTC positioning, VIX term structure) were not researched.
- Exact count of unexplained moves in Fair (2002) and in Cutler et al. not obtained (full texts not read).

## 5. Cadence for long-horizon investors and avoiding over-reaction

### Takeaway
Documented institutional cadence is calendar-driven for the shared layer (daily exchange, weekly commentary, semi-annual outlook), while guidance on single-stock action is to tie reviews to pre-written thesis facts rather than price moves. Formal evidence on cooling-off rules is thin; what exists is practitioner guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Daily: Wellington's Morning Meeting is every business day, with notes and replay relayed across regions. — [Wellington PDF](https://www.wellington.com/content/dam/wellington/pdf/en/morning-meeting-v7.pdf)
- Weekly and semi-annual: BII weekly commentary; Outlook Forum twice a year producing the Global Outlook and Midyear Outlook. *(search summary only)* — [BlackRock Investment Institute](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/insights/blackrock-investment-institute/insights); [BII Midyear Outlook 2026](https://www.blackrock.com/us/individual/literature/whitepaper/bii-midyear-outlook-2026.pdf)
- Monthly per-stock refresh in a deployed LLM system: MarketSenseAI's synthesis agent issues a monthly thesis and recommendation per stock. — [Fatouros and Metaxas, arXiv 2604.17327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17327)
- Practitioner guidance (not institutional studies): review triggers and invalidation criteria should be written before purchase and tied to facts and events, not daily price movement; a falling price does not by itself break a thesis; one checklist proposes 24 hours between idea and order and waiting a day after a large move before re-running the checklist; keep a decision journal and review the journal at scheduled checks. *(search summary only; mixed-quality sources)* — [Morningstar Australia, Investor Playbook: When to sell](https://www.morningstar.com.au/insights/personal-finance/6VFQSZW4NFB63PIHB3KM6ON5YU/investor-playbook-when-sell-your-investment); [Kubera, Investment thesis](https://www.kubera.com/blog/investment-thesis)
- Writing the rationale acts as a circuit breaker, and ending it with a "review" price rather than a "target" price is recommended. *(search summary only; the search attributed this to a Morgan Stanley Investment Management piece — attribution not verified)* — [Morningstar India, Why we write our investing rationale](https://www.morningstar.in/posts/34572/)
- Statistical support for waiting: volatility stays elevated for at least two weeks around low-clarity jumps, and media-pessimism price pressure reverts. — [NBER WP 28687 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28687/w28687.pdf); [Tetlock 2007](https://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock_Media_Sentiment_JF.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested split for the bot (inference): **scheduled** — weekly market-environment note (BII weekly analogue), monthly or quarterly per-stock thesis check (MarketSenseAI monthly; earnings calendar), annual exposure-table refresh; **event-triggered** — a composite-layer run when a market-level jump threshold is crossed (Baker et al. use 2.5% for the US index, 2–4% elsewhere depending on volatility), and a per-stock run on company-scope events (8-K-class) or a stock move that is large *relative to its sector/market*.
- On a broad sell-off day the trigger logic should fire the composite layer once and suppress per-stock heavy reviews unless a stock's move is idiosyncratic (excess over market/sector) or a company-scope event exists. Per-stock output on such a day can be a one-line "market-driven, thesis items unchanged" statement generated from the composite note.
- "Do nothing" as default follows from sections 4 and 5: explanation is unreliable on day one, volatility is elevated around unclear jumps, and tone-driven pressure reverts. The action trigger should be a changed thesis fact, never the narrative.
- A settled second pass (e.g. after 2 weeks, matching the Baker et al. volatility window) is a defensible cooling-off length; this number is an adaptation, not a documented rule.

### Gaps
- No formal institutional study or firm policy document was found on cooling-off periods, "do nothing" defaults, or the return effect of pre-commitment; evidence is practitioner writing.
- CFA Institute material on IPS review cadence and investment-committee meeting frequency was not retrieved.
- No source found on how large managers set stock-specific event triggers (e.g. relative-move thresholds).

## 6. Multi-agent LLM research systems (2024–2026) separating macro/market from per-stock agents

### Takeaway
Published systems mostly put macro inside a per-ticker pipeline (TradingAgents' News Analyst covers macro for each ticker run) or as one of several specialists feeding a per-stock synthesiser (MarketSenseAI); none retrieved documents a shared market layer computed once and routed by exposure. Reported weaknesses centre on information loss in long agent conversations, cost per decision, and evaluation flaws (look-ahead bias, short backtests) rather than on duplicated macro work, which is not discussed.

### Cited Findings
- **TradingAgents** (Xiao, Sun, Luo, Wang; arXiv 2412.20138): roles are fundamentals, sentiment, news and technical analysts; bull and bear researchers who debate with a facilitator; trader; three-perspective risk team; fund manager. The News Analyst reviews "news articles, government announcements, and macroeconomic indicators to assess the macro state, world events, and company changes" — macro is inside the per-run analyst team; no separate market-level layer is described. — [arXiv 2412.20138 (HTML v4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138v4)
- TradingAgents communication design: analysts and traders pass **structured reports**; free dialogue is confined to researcher and risk debates, whose outcome is stored as a structured entry. Reason given: pure natural-language chains suffer "a 'telephone effect', where details are lost, and states become corrupted as conversations lengthen", plus context-length forgetting. — [arXiv 2412.20138 (HTML v4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138v4)
- TradingAgents cost and evaluation limits: 11 LLM calls and 20+ tool calls per prediction; backtest only 1 Jan–29 Mar 2024 on AAPL, GOOGL, AMZN; the authors note the top Sharpe ratio "exceeds our expected empirical range" and attribute it to few pullbacks in the window. — [arXiv 2412.20138 (HTML v4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138v4)
- **MarketSenseAI** (Fatouros and Metaxas, arXiv 2604.17327, Apr 2026): four specialists — News, Fundamentals, Dynamics, Macro — feed a synthesis agent that issues a monthly thesis and recommendation per stock; signals generated live at each date to avoid look-ahead bias. Reported: S&P 500 strong-buy equal-weight +2.18%/month vs +1.15% for passive equal-weight over 19 months (99.7th percentile of 10,000 random portfolios, p=0.003); S&P 100 result over 35 months not formally significant. Agent contribution shifts with regime: Fundamentals leads on S&P 500, Macro on S&P 100, Dynamics episodic. (Abstract-level; authors' own system.) — [arXiv 2604.17327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17327)

### Inferences
- The owner's problem (each per-stock review repeating market research) is exactly TradingAgents' structure: the News Analyst re-does macro for every ticker run. The repo memory notes TradingAgents was already backtested locally; these notes add that the paper itself offers no shared-layer remedy.
- MarketSenseAI is the nearest precedent for "macro agent + per-stock synthesis", but its abstract does not say whether the macro output is computed once and reused across stocks. The composite layer the owner proposes (computed once, routed by exposure tags) appears to be ahead of what these papers document, so it should be treated as own design, justified by the human-firm precedent in section 1 rather than by LLM literature.
- Transferable design rules: (1) pass a **structured** composite note between layers, not chat transcripts (TradingAgents' telephone-effect finding); (2) give the per-stock layer the note as a fixed, dated input so it cannot re-research or re-interpret the market; (3) for cause attribution use independent drafts plus a reconciling supervisor and explicit probabilities (AIA Forecaster pattern) rather than debate-to-consensus, since consensus rounds can converge on a shared hallucinated cause (the hallucination risk AlphaAgents names); (4) keep an "unknown" outcome.
- Narrative overfitting risk is supported indirectly: look-ahead bias means an LLM "explaining" a past move may be recalling the later accepted story. For live use this matters less, but any backtest of the composite layer on historical sell-offs would be contaminated.
